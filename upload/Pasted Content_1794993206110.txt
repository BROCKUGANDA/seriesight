# PART B: PRODUCT VISION AND PROBLEM

## B1. Product vision

Professional esports coaching depends on turning large volumes of match data into a small number of decisions that can change the outcome of a series.

Coaches must understand:
- Which champions an opponent prioritizes.
- Which champions individual players consistently perform with.
- How pick and ban preferences change by side.
- Which strategies become predictable across games.
- Which match patterns are repeatable and which are isolated.
- How a Fearless Draft series changes the available champion pool.
- How to adapt when an opponent changes its strategy.
- Which conclusions are supported by sufficient evidence.

Seriesight turns these tasks into a unified coaching workflow.

The product should feel like an advanced sports intelligence terminal, not a chatbot, spreadsheet replacement with decorative cards, or generic AI analytics dashboard.

## B2. The problem

Existing preparation processes can involve disconnected spreadsheets, manually assembled reports, fragmented match histories and draft documents.

The result is:
- Duplicate manual work.
- Inconsistent statistics.
- Difficulty recognizing patterns across multiple games.
- Poor visibility into evidence quality.
- Draft preparation that does not account for changing restrictions across a series.
- Limited ability to compare alternative strategies.

## B3. The solution

Seriesight combines five capabilities:

1. Historical match intelligence.
2. Opponent behavioral profiling.
3. Macro strategy analysis.
4. Sequential pick/ban and Fearless Draft simulation.
5. Evidence-backed coaching recommendations.

The central product principle:

**Every recommendation must answer three questions: What should the coach consider? Why? How strong is the evidence?**

## B4. Target users

### Head Coach
Needs a high-level opponent overview, strategic priorities, alternative draft plans and a complete preparation workspace.

### Draft Coach
Needs pick/ban sequencing, champion availability, role flexibility, counter strategies and Fearless Draft simulations.

### Match Analyst
Needs match ingestion, filters, historical tendencies, source verification, sample sizes and exportable preparation materials where licensing permits.

### Performance Analyst
Needs trends, macro indicators, objective timing, player-specific aggregate performance and consistency measurements.

### Team Manager
Needs access to preparation status, completed reports, upcoming series and workspace coordination.

## B5. Product positioning

Seriesight is a pre-match professional coaching and analysis tool.

It does not:
- Replace the official game client.
- Execute game actions.
- Inject information into an active game.
- Provide cheating functionality.
- Automatically lock picks or dictate a coach's final decision.
- Claim access to information not provided by its authorized data sources.

It presents multiple possible strategies and makes the supporting evidence visible.

### Fearless modes

Support these independently configurable rules:

**Standard Draft**
Champions are available according to ordinary tournament pick/ban and same-game restrictions.

**Hard/Full Fearless**
A champion picked by either team in an earlier game is unavailable for either team in subsequent games of the series.

**Soft Fearless**
A champion picked by one team in an earlier game is unavailable to that same team in later games, while the opposing team's previous picks remain available subject to other rules.

**Custom Tournament Rules**
Allow the tournament to define:
- Pick restrictions.
- Ban restrictions.
- Whether restrictions apply to both teams or separately.
- When restrictions reset.
- Best-of-series format.
- Draft turn sequence.

Do not assume that a tournament's use of the word Fearless implies identical rules across every competition.

Banned champions, picked champions and previously selected champions must be tracked as separate states. Do not incorrectly treat bans as completed picks.

### Draft state machine

Use an explicit deterministic state machine.

States:
- CONFIGURATION
- BAN_PHASE_1
- PICK_PHASE_1
- BAN_PHASE_2
- PICK_PHASE_2
- FINALIZATION
- COMPLETED
- INVALID
- CANCELLED

Every tournament ruleset must define its valid phase sequence and action order. The draft engine must not assume a fixed sequence without a ruleset configuration.

Validate every action before changing state.

Invalid actions must not partially modify the board.

### Pick and ban actions

Users can:
- Search champions.
- Select a champion.
- Lock a pick.
- Assign a player or role.
- Remove or undo the last action if permitted.
- Inspect champion history.
- View the reason a champion is unavailable.

The engine must prevent:
- Duplicate picks where prohibited.
- Picking a previously restricted champion.
- Invalid bans.
- Actions in an incorrect phase.
- Actions from the wrong side.
- Actions out of sequence.
- Completion without required selections.

Every action generates an event with a unique ID, timestamp, actor, team, phase and previous state version.

- Frontline capability.
- Crowd control.
- Engage.
- Disengage.
- Scaling.
- Siege.
- Objective setup.
- Teamfight profile.
- Role coverage.

**Champion Relationships**
- Historical synergies.
- Opponent matchup information.
- Flex-pick possibilities.
- Role alternatives.
- Historical sample sizes.

**Recommendation ranking**

Recommend:
- Three potential picks.
- Three potential bans.
- Two alternative compositions where sufficient data exists.

Every recommendation includes:
- Candidate champion.
- Score.
- Why it is suggested.
- Underlying observations.
- Sample count.
- Patch relevance.
- Risks and trade-offs.
- Alternative.

Never present a recommendation as an instruction to lock a champion.

# PART I: ANALYTICS AND RECOMMENDATION ENGINE

## I1. Analytical philosophy

The analytics engine must be deterministic, reproducible and explainable.

An LLM must never be the source of the underlying statistical truth.

Use code for:
- Frequency calculations.
- Pick and ban rates.
- Sample sizes.
- Confidence intervals.
- Composition scoring.
- Restriction tracking.
- Series simulation.

AI may optionally write a concise explanation of an already calculated finding.

## I2. Pick and ban frequency

For any champion:

Pick rate = Number of eligible picks / Number of eligible games or opportunities.

Ban rate = Number of eligible bans / Number of eligible draft opportunities.

The denominator must be explicitly defined.

Do not mix:
- Game-based pick rate.
- Draft-slot-based priority.
- Team-specific pick rate.
- Player-specific usage.
- Champion availability.

Each requires its own metric definition.

## I3. Sample-size handling

Use Wilson confidence intervals for binomial rates where appropriate.

Use a documented Bayesian Beta prior or another defensible method for smoothing small samples.

Display:
- Observations.
- Eligible opportunities.
- Estimated rate.
- Confidence interval.
- Data coverage.

Never call a metric "high confidence" merely because it is visually impressive.

Implement configurable confidence labels based on sample size and interval width.

## I4. Recency

Use configurable recency weighting.

Example:
- Recent patch data has the greatest relevance.
- Older patch data is retained for historical comparisons when permitted.
- Significant patch transitions reduce the relevance of old draft observations.

A possible exponential decay function:

`weight = exp(-ln(2) * age / halfLife)`

Document the half-life and test that the weighting behaves as intended.

## I5. Draft recommendation score

Create a transparent 0–100 draft suitability score.

Potential components:
- Opponent priority.
- Historical champion usage.
- Relevant draft-side context.
- Champion availability.
- Team composition fit.
- Flexibility.
- Evidence quality.
- Matchup support.

Every weight must be configurable and documented.

Separate the score into visible components.

Avoid claiming this score is a win probability.

## I6. Recommendation output contract

Each recommendation must contain:

- champion_id
- candidate_name
- recommendation_type
- score
- score_breakdown
- explanation
- supporting_evidence
- sample_size
- confidence_interval
- patch_context
- positive_factors
- risk_factors
- alternatives

No result without a source or explicit indication that the result is based on a fixture or coach input.

## I7. Composition intelligence

Develop composition feature calculations.

Initial dimensions:
- Engage.
- Disengage.
- Frontline.
- Crowd control.
- Physical damage.
- Magic damage.
- Scaling.
- Siege.
- Pick potential.
- Objective control.

Champion attribute data must be sourced from defensible metadata or explicitly coach-curated mappings.

Do not assume all champion attributes can be inferred accurately from champion names.

If a dimension is unknown, mark it as unknown and exclude it from the score rather than inventing a value.

## I8. Macro analysis

Implement independent metric calculators.

Each calculator should include:
- Input requirements.
- Formula.
- Null behavior.
- Edge cases.
- Tests.
- Output provenance.

Example:
First objective acquisition rate must be based on valid objective events, not on the final match winner.

If the event sequence is unavailable, the metric must remain unavailable unless a valid source provides a direct value.

    54→# PART B: PRODUCT VISION AND PROBLEM
    55→
    56→## B1. Product vision
    57→
    58→Professional esports coaching depends on turning large volumes of match data into a small number of decisions that can change the outcome of a series.
    59→
    60→Coaches must understand:
    61→- Which champions an opponent prioritizes.
    62→- Which champions individual players consistently perform with.
    63→- How pick and ban preferences change by side.
    64→- Which strategies become predictable across games.
    65→- Which match patterns are repeatable and which are isolated.
    66→- How a Fearless Draft series changes the available champion pool.
    67→- How to adapt when an opponent changes its strategy.
    68→- Which conclusions are supported by sufficient evidence.
    69→
    70→Seriesight turns these tasks into a unified coaching workflow.
    71→
    72→The product should feel like an advanced sports intelligence terminal, not a chatbot, spreadsheet replacement with decorative cards, or generic AI analytics dashboard.
    73→
    74→## B2. The problem
    75→
    76→Existing preparation processes can involve disconnected spreadsheets, manually assembled reports, fragmented match histories and draft documents.
    77→
    78→The result is:
    79→- Duplicate manual work.
    80→- Inconsistent statistics.
    81→- Difficulty recognizing patterns across multiple games.
    82→- Poor visibility into evidence quality.
    83→- Draft preparation that does not account for changing restrictions across a series.
    84→- Limited ability to compare alternative strategies.
    85→
    86→## B3. The solution
    87→
    88→Seriesight combines five capabilities:
    89→
    90→1. Historical match intelligence.
    91→2. Opponent behavioral profiling.
    92→3. Macro strategy analysis.
    93→4. Sequential pick/ban and Fearless Draft simulation.
    94→5. Evidence-backed coaching recommendations.
    95→
    96→The central product principle:
    97→
    98→**Every recommendation must answer three questions: What should the coach consider? Why? How strong is the evidence?**
    99→
   100→## B4. Target users
   101→
   102→### Head Coach
   103→Needs a high-level opponent overview, strategic priorities, alternative draft plans and a complete preparation workspace.
   104→
   105→### Draft Coach
   106→Needs pick/ban sequencing, champion availability, role flexibility, counter strategies and Fearless Draft simulations.
   107→
   108→### Match Analyst
   109→Needs match ingestion, filters, historical tendencies, source verification, sample sizes and exportable preparation materials where licensing permits.
   110→
   111→### Performance Analyst
   112→Needs trends, macro indicators, objective timing, player-specific aggregate performance and consistency measurements.
   113→
   114→### Team Manager
   115→Needs access to preparation status, completed reports, upcoming series and workspace coordination.
   116→
   117→## B5. Product positioning
   118→
   119→Seriesight is a pre-match professional coaching and analysis tool.
   120→
   121→It does not:
   122→- Replace the official game client.
   123→- Execute game actions.
   124→- Inject information into an active game.
   125→- Provide cheating functionality.
   126→- Automatically lock picks or dictate a coach's final decision.
   127→- Claim access to information not provided by its authorized data sources.
   128→
   129→It presents multiple possible strategies and makes the supporting evidence visible.

   459→### Fearless modes
   460→
   461→Support these independently configurable rules:
   462→
   463→**Standard Draft**
   464→Champions are available according to ordinary tournament pick/ban and same-game restrictions.
   465→
   466→**Hard/Full Fearless**
   467→A champion picked by either team in an earlier game is unavailable for either team in subsequent games of the series.
   468→
   469→**Soft Fearless**
   470→A champion picked by one team in an earlier game is unavailable to that same team in later games, while the opposing team's previous picks remain available subject to other rules.
   471→
   472→**Custom Tournament Rules**
   473→Allow the tournament to define:
   474→- Pick restrictions.
   475→- Ban restrictions.
   476→- Whether restrictions apply to both teams or separately.
   477→- When restrictions reset.
   478→- Best-of-series format.
   479→- Draft turn sequence.
   480→
   481→Do not assume that a tournament's use of the word Fearless implies identical rules across every competition.
   482→
   483→Banned champions, picked champions and previously selected champions must be tracked as separate states. Do not incorrectly treat bans as completed picks.
   484→
   485→### Draft state machine
   486→
   487→Use an explicit deterministic state machine.
   488→
   489→States:
   490→- CONFIGURATION
   491→- BAN_PHASE_1
   492→- PICK_PHASE_1
   493→- BAN_PHASE_2
   494→- PICK_PHASE_2
   495→- FINALIZATION
   496→- COMPLETED
   497→- INVALID
   498→- CANCELLED
   499→
   500→Every tournament ruleset must define its valid phase sequence and action order. The draft engine must not assume a fixed sequence without a ruleset configuration.
   501→
   502→Validate every action before changing state.
   503→
   504→Invalid actions must not partially modify the board.
   505→
   506→### Pick and ban actions
   507→
   508→Users can:
   509→- Search champions.
   510→- Select a champion.
   511→- Lock a pick.
   512→- Assign a player or role.
   513→- Remove or undo the last action if permitted.
   514→- Inspect champion history.
   515→- View the reason a champion is unavailable.
   516→
   517→The engine must prevent:
   518→- Duplicate picks where prohibited.
   519→- Picking a previously restricted champion.
   520→- Invalid bans.
   521→- Actions in an incorrect phase.
   522→- Actions from the wrong side.
   523→- Actions out of sequence.
   524→- Completion without required selections.
   525→
   526→Every action generates an event with a unique ID, timestamp, actor, team, phase and previous state version.
   527→
   528→### Draft analysis
   529→
   530→Display:
   531→
   532→**Composition Balance**
   533→- Physical and magic damage distribution where classifiable.
   534→- Frontline capability.
   535→- Crowd control.
   536→- Engage.
   537→- Disengage.
   538→- Scaling.
   539→- Siege.
   540→- Objective setup.
   541→- Teamfight profile.
   542→- Role coverage.
   543→
   544→**Champion Relationships**
   545→- Historical synergies.
   546→- Opponent matchup information.
   547→- Flex-pick possibilities.
   548→- Role alternatives.
   549→- Historical sample sizes.
   550→
   551→**Recommendation ranking**
   552→
   553→Recommend:
   554→- Three potential picks.
   555→- Three potential bans.
   556→- Two alternative compositions where sufficient data exists.
   557→
   558→Every recommendation includes:
   559→- Candidate champion.
   560→- Score.
   561→- Why it is suggested.
   562→- Underlying observations.
   563→- Sample count.
   564→- Patch relevance.
   565→- Risks and trade-offs.
   566→- Alternative.
   567→
   568→Never present a recommendation as an instruction to lock a champion.

  1468→# PART I: ANALYTICS AND RECOMMENDATION ENGINE
  1469→
  1470→## I1. Analytical philosophy
  1471→
  1472→The analytics engine must be deterministic, reproducible and explainable.
  1473→
  1474→An LLM must never be the source of the underlying statistical truth.
  1475→
  1476→Use code for:
  1477→- Frequency calculations.
  1478→- Pick and ban rates.
  1479→- Sample sizes.
  1480→- Confidence intervals.
  1481→- Composition scoring.
  1482→- Restriction tracking.
  1483→- Series simulation.
  1484→
  1485→AI may optionally write a concise explanation of an already calculated finding.
  1486→
  1487→## I2. Pick and ban frequency
  1488→
  1489→For any champion:
  1490→
  1491→Pick rate = Number of eligible picks / Number of eligible games or opportunities.
  1492→
  1493→Ban rate = Number of eligible bans / Number of eligible draft opportunities.
  1494→
  1495→The denominator must be explicitly defined.
  1496→
  1497→Do not mix:
  1498→- Game-based pick rate.
